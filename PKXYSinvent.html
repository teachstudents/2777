Synonym Scan (Lexical Competence)

📍 Inventions.pdf • Page 12

"There have been millions of inventions over the years. Some of them, like the invention of the lightbulb, are well known. However, others are obscure."

Read the paragraph above. Find a single word that has the exact opposite meaning of the phrase "well known."

Reveal Teacher Key & Strategy
Target Answer:

obscure

The First Step in Identifying a Synonym (or Antonym) Like "Obscure"
The absolute first step is to locate the logical transition signal or "pivot word" in the sentence.

Before trying to guess a word's meaning, students must look for structural signposts like "However," "But," "Although," or "On the other hand." These words act like a balancing scale. Once students spot "However," they know the author is pivoting to a contrasting idea. They can then identify the known anchor word ("well known") on one side of the scale and logically deduce that the target word ("obscure") on the other side must mean the opposite.

Pedagogical Target: Evaluates selective context scanning and direct antonym deduction using contrast transition clues.

Pronoun Reference (Grammatical Cohesion)

📍 Inventions.pdf • Page 9

"Scientists needed to invent ways for people to breathe in places where there was no air. They needed to invent new materials to keep both humans and spacecraft safe from extreme temperatures."

Look at Paragraph 3 on page 9. What specific noun does the pronoun "They" at the beginning of the second sentence refer to?

Reveal Teacher Key & Strategy
Target Answer:

Scientists

The Pronoun Antecedent "Substitution Rule" Explained

The Substitution Rule is a step-by-step grammatical test designed to help students track pronoun referents across sentence boundaries without guessing.

  • Step 1: Identify "Candidate Nouns": Write down all the plural (or singular, matching the pronoun's number) nouns in the preceding sentence. For example, in the sentence: "Scientists needed to invent ways for people to breathe in places where there was no air," the candidate nouns for the pronoun "They" are:
    • Scientists
    • Ways
    • People
    • Places
  • Step 2: Perform the Swap: Rewrite the target sentence by replacing the pronoun "They" with each candidate noun:
    • Option A: Ways needed to invent...
    • Option B: People needed to invent...
    • Option C: Places needed to invent...
    • Option D: Scientists needed to invent...
  • Step 3: Test for Semantic Sense: Eliminate options that are physically or logically impossible. Ways and places cannot perform the action of inventing. While people is grammatically possible, the text specifically identifies scientists as the specific group responsible for these material breakthroughs. Thus, Scientists is the verified antecedent.

Pedagogical Target: Tests syntactic tracking and the ability to bypass nearby "distractor" plural nouns (ways, people, places) using the Substitution Rule.

Fact Validation (True / False / Not Given)

📍 Inventions.pdf • Page 16

"In 1944, two inventors worked together to build an even bigger and better computer. It was 55 feet long and 8 feet high. It filled a giant room and weighed 5 tons... It used lots of energy and needed a way to help keep it cool."

Read the passage above. Decide if the following statement is True (T), False (F), or Not Given (NG) based strictly on what is written:

Statement: The 5-ton computer built in 1944 was kept cool by blowing cold air through large metal vents.

Reveal Teacher Key & Strategy
Target Answer:

Not Given

Pedagogical Target: Tests critical evaluation. While the text states the computer "needed a way to help keep it cool," it never names the specific method (such as metal vents or cold air). Students must select "Not Given" and resist applying real-world logical assumptions.
